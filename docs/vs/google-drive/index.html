SQUARES Vault vs Google Drive Alone

Google Drive is where your files live; SQUARES Vault is the intelligent filing clerk who reads them, tracks deadlines, and pings you before they expire.

The Core Problem with Folders Alone

Almost every household starts with good intentions: creating a folder hierarchy in Google Drive called Documents/Car, Documents/Insurance, and Documents/Warranties.

Within three months, the system breaks down:

  • Filing Friction: Uploading requires saving the PDF from WhatsApp, opening Drive, navigating through 4 nested folders, and renaming IMG_20261010_12345.jpg manually. In reality, documents stay stranded in phone downloads.
  • Passive Storage: Google Drive stores files safely, but it cannot read a faded PUC slip or an insurance schedule. It will never tell you that your pollution certificate expires next Tuesday.
  • No Expiry Clock: Files in Drive have an "upload date" and "modified date", but no concept of a regulatory "expiry date" or "renewal deadline".

Why You Don't Have to Choose Between Them

SQUARES Vault was explicitly built not to lock you into a proprietary cloud silo.

During onboarding, SQUARES Vault connects to your Google Drive using the strictly scoped https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file permission. This means:

  • SQUARES Vault creates an organized SQUARES Vault directory in your personal Google Drive.
  • Original document files (images, PDFs) upload directly to your Drive.
  • SQUARES Vault cannot read, alter, or access any other files, photos, or folders in your Google Drive.
  • If you ever stop using SQUARES Vault, your files remain completely intact in your Google Drive. You lose nothing.
